Why This Game?

The game is not a one-time experience. The Amalgamator field editor turns every player into a potential creator. Combined with Discord integration and the upcoming online sharing system, content flows from players back into the game indefinitely. The Intro Version is already live. The foundation is built. What's missing is the marketing push to bridge from early adopters to a self-sustaining community.

Three difficulty modes (Flawless, Revival, Immortal) ensure anyone can play — from rhythm veterans to first-time VR users. This broadens the audience beyond a single niche.

The codebase is designed for expansion. Adding new content doesn't require rewriting systems. This keeps development agile and responsive to player feedback.

Marketing as a Skill

I treat marketing like any other skill I've learned — programming, debugging, systems design. It's improvable, replicable, and systematic. It has patterns, best practices, known failure modes, and room for iteration. I've spent time studying what works and what doesn't, specifically for rhythm games, VR, and user-generated content.

I also study the failures. The indie graveyard is full of good games that never got seen. I don't want to join it. So I learn from both successes and mistakes. Not every campaign will convert. Not every channel will deliver. That's fine — I'm not looking for a silver bullet. I'm looking for a systematic approach: test, measure, double down on what works, cut what doesn't. Just like writing code.

Hardware Upgrades

$3,000 – $5,000

VR development is demanding, and Unreal Engine 5 doesn't go easy on hardware. These upgrades are about keeping workflow smooth and avoiding constant waiting.

  • GPU: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 ($1,600 – $2,000). VR needs stable, high frame rates — stutter breaks immersion and makes testing uncomfortable. User-generated fields can get complex, and a 4090 gives enough headroom to handle them without dropping frames.
  • CPU: AMD Ryzen 9 7950X or Intel i9-14900K ($500 – $700). Faster compilation means less time staring at progress bars. When iterating on gameplay or debugging, those seconds add up fast.
  • RAM: 64GB DDR5 ($200 – $350). Running UE5, Visual Studio, profiling tools, and VR runtime simultaneously eats memory. 64GB keeps everything running side by side without bottlenecks.
  • Storage: 2TB NVMe SSD ($150 – $300). Faster builds and asset loading mean less downtime between test runs.
  • VR Headset: Valve Index or Meta Quest Pro ($1,000 – $1,500). Testing VR gameplay requires consistent access to a reliable headset.

Beyond speed, better hardware reduces crash risks. When the system isn't constantly pushing its limits, stability improves. Fewer crashes mean fewer interruptions and more productive development sessions.
