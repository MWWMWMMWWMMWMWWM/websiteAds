Hardware Upgrades

$2,000 – $3,000

VR development is demanding, and Unreal Engine 5 doesn't go easy on hardware. These upgrades are about keeping workflow smooth and avoiding constant waiting.

  • GPU: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 or 4090 ($1,200 – $1,800). VR needs stable, high frame rates—stutter breaks immersion and makes testing uncomfortable. User-generated fields can get complex, and a 4090 gives enough headroom to handle them without dropping frames.
  • CPU: AMD Ryzen 9 7900X or Intel i9-13900K ($400 – $600). Faster compilation means less time staring at progress bars. When iterating on gameplay or debugging, those seconds add up fast.
  • RAM: 64GB DDR5 ($200 – $300). Running UE5, Visual Studio, profiling tools, and VR runtime simultaneously eats memory. 64GB keeps everything running side by side without bottlenecks.
  • Storage: 2TB NVMe SSD ($150 – $250). Faster builds and asset loading mean less downtime between test runs.

Beyond speed, better hardware reduces crash risks. When the system isn't constantly pushing its limits, stability improves. Fewer crashes mean fewer interruptions and more productive development sessions.
